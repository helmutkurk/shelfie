It started with a real request

“Can you check the competition in a few shops for me?”

Alex wants to bring a new grocery product to Germany. Before launching, he needs to know what similar products cost and whether they are really on the shelf. He lives abroad, and no AI can walk into a supermarket and look.

The idea

An agent that hires a person to look.

People nearby, or a worker in the supermarket, photograph the price tag with a one-time hand sign. A verifier agent checks the photo before anyone is paid.

The payment

Nobody pays for a photo that isn't true.

The fee waits in Cardano escrow. Photo passes: the person is paid and the report is delivered. No passing photo: the brand gets its money back.

Entering an unfamiliar market means finding someone to visit stores, photograph competitors and read their shelf prices. A brand needs evidence it can assess without knowing the person behind the camera.

How it works

Proof first, payment second.

  1. 1

    Order

    A brand picks one product and a Munich store from our catalog. The fee is locked in Masumi escrow on Cardano.

  2. 2

    Claim

    The task appears for people in the city. The first one to claim it walks to the store.

  3. 3

    Photograph

    Live camera only. A hand sign next to the price tag, shown only when the camera opens, challenges the checker to take a fresh photo.

  4. 4

    Verify

    A separate verifier agent, paid per check over x402, reads product and price; fixed rules decide pass or fail.

  5. 5

    Settle

    Pass: the person is paid on-chain and the report is delivered. No passing photo in time: the brand is refunded.

Why trust: the blockchain

Proof first, payment second.

Escrow movements follow contract rules on Cardano. Verifier fees and checker payouts also leave public receipts; the photo verdict still comes from Shelfie's verifier.

  • Locked before work starts.

    The brand's fee sits in a Masumi escrow contract on Cardano. It is released by the contract's rules: a result is submitted, or the buyer reclaims funds after the unlock time. Both buyer and seller accounts belong to Shelfie in this demo.

  • Released on a passing result.

    A passing photo means Shelfie submits the result and the escrow can settle. No passing photo before the window ends: nothing is submitted and the buyer can reclaim the fee after the unlock time (about 30 minutes after the lock in our test run).

  • Every check is a receipt.

    The verifier is sold per check over x402. Each check is a visible on-chain payment, pass or fail.

  • Checkable beyond our database.

    Fund movements can be checked on the public ledger. The verdict itself still rests on the verifier, and the photo stays off-chain.

Limits: prototype on a test network. The demo runs both sides of the verifier payment. The hand sign challenges freshness; it cannot prove location or stop every fake. One accepted photo records observed presence and price, not placement.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is this real money?

No. Shelfie runs on Cardano preprod, a public test network. All amounts are test ADA with no monetary value, and the EUR figures on this page are illustrative.

Why a blockchain?

Masumi escrow locks funds under contract rules: the seller submits a result, or the buyer can reclaim the fee after the unlock time if no result arrives. Payments are publicly checkable. Shelfie still operates the verifier and decides whether to submit the result. Whether brands want to deal in ADA is still open.

What if the photo fails?

Nothing is paid to the checker, who can retry with a new hand sign while attempts and time remain. If no photo passes before the window ends, the result is withheld and the buyer can reclaim the fee after the unlock time. Our refund test took about 30 minutes from the lock, with the engine on fakes and no photo.

Who sees my photo?

The server stores it, and the verifier and its vision model read it. The brand receives the result. See the privacy notice for details and retention.

Can it be faked?

Not easily, but it is possible. The live-camera flow, one-time hand sign and duplicate checks deter reuse, but a printout with the right hand sign could pass. A screen photo fails only if the model flags it; browser location can be spoofed. Limits in detail.

Who is it for?

Brands that need eyes in a market where they know nobody, and people nearby who can photograph a shelf. Today: one Munich store per job, on a test network.
