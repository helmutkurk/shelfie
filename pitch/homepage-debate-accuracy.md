## 2. Technical overstatements in homepage-content-tech.md

1. Card 1: "Neither Shelfie nor the brand can touch it while the job runs." Masumi lets the buyer request a refund and the seller withdraw after an unlock time. Say: "locked in a contract; released by its rules (result submitted, or refund)". Also state that in the demo the buyer and the seller are both Shelfie accounts (ADR-20); "the brand's fee" is a Sokosumi test buyer wallet, not a brand.
2. Card 2 / FAQ: "refunds the brand automatically". The measured refund (`ca8f5de9`) took about 30 minutes and ran with the engine on fakes and no photo; whether the refund is triggered without a buyer-side action is not shown in the evidence. Say "refunded to the buyer (about 30 minutes in our test run)".
3. Card 3: "cost of verification ... cannot be hidden". The operator controls both wallets in the demo, so the fee being on-chain proves a payment happened, not that it was independent. Say "each check is a visible on-chain payment". The only x402 receipts (`cbc9cb5d`, `1547a4a7`) had a stub verdict; the page labels this, keep it in the row, not only in a footnote.
4. Card 4: "can be checked by the brand without trusting Shelfie". True for fund movements only. The pass/fail verdict comes from Shelfie's own verifier (operated by Shelfie in the demo) and the photo is off-chain. Add: "the verdict itself still rests on the verifier".

## 3. Legal fixes

**Price-compliance wording (DE/EU).** Resale price maintenance is prohibited (section 1 GWB, Art. 101 TFEU); an RPM-flavoured use case ("check that retailers keep the agreed price") is a risk to the brand customer and to our credibility. Final: "Shelfie checks what is on the shelf: that the product is there, what the shelf tag says, and that a promotion you paid for is visible. It does not set or enforce prices." Avoid "agreed price", "price compliance", "enforce", "violations by retailers". Competitor price observation is fine, but say "publicly displayed shelf prices". Also avoid "promotion compliance" implying audits of the retailer's contract; use "promotion visible on the shelf".

**Impressum (section 5 DDG).** Opened the law text today. Required: name and address of the provider (a street address where documents can be served), electronic contact including an email address, plus register, VAT and supervisory items only where they apply (none do). Corrections to the draft: (a) section 5 DDG does not require a phone or a contact form as a "second quick contact"; an email address plus a fast reply is the statutory minimum (the draft's "required by section 5 DDG" is overstated; a second channel is still advisable); (b) section 5 DDG applies to services offered "geschaeftsmaessig, in der Regel gegen Entgelt"; a non-commercial student prototype may be outside it, but an Impressum is still the safe and expected choice for a public page shown to judges; (c) section 18 (2) MStV applies only to journalistic-editorial content; keep the responsible person line only if the page has such content, otherwise drop it; (d) a TUM institute or a c/o address must not be used unless TUM agrees; (e) full legal names must come from the team, not from the story page; (f) do not name "Los Curiosos" as a legal entity (it is not one; say "team"). Natural persons jointly (GbR-like) means all four names and one shared service address.

**Privacy notice (Art. 13 GDPR checklist).** Present: controller identity and contact, purposes and legal bases, recipients (Google, Vercel, Cloudflare), third-country transfer, retention, rights, withdrawal of consent, complaint authority, no automated decision. Missing or weak: (1) whether providing data is a legal/contractual requirement and the consequence of declining (partly in "Declining"; add the Art. 13(2)(e) sentence); (2) Art. 13(2)(f) automated decision-making: the verifier pass/fail drives payment; state "the verification decision is automated by fixed rules; there is no human review in this prototype; you may contact us to have a person look at a result" (Art. 22 risk is low because payout is test money, but say it); (3) transfer mechanism placeholder for Google and (if used) OpenAI: Google LLC is on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework list but verify the current listing and that the Gemini API terms in use (free vs paid) are the ones assumed; (4) recipients: add the Masumi and Sokosumi services and the Cardano network (public); (5) legal basis for hosting logs is fine; (6) the section "Photos ... if a person is visible, the verifier records it as a note": a person in a photo is third-party data; tell workers to avoid people and say what happens when one appears; (7) children: workers must be adults (add to Terms); (8) retention dates are "planned", write them as commitments only after the team confirms; (9) Art. 26 joint controllers: say the essence of the arrangement is available on request; (10) location consent text must match what the app shows ("Google or OpenAI" in the consent text is wrong, only Google is configured). Consent is the right basis for location; photos delivered for payment fit Art. 6(1)(b) only if the worker is a counterparty, which conflicts with the Terms' "not a work relationship"; use consent for photos and keep (b) only for the payout address, or simply say "consent" for both.

**BayLDA address.** Verified today on lda.bayern.de: Bayerisches Landesamt fuer Datenschutzaufsicht, Promenade 18, 91522 Ansbach (visitor address); postal address Postfach 1349, 91504 Ansbach. Draft is correct. Add "Postfach 1349, 91504 Ansbach" for mail. Source: https://www.lda.bayern.de/de/kontakt.html

**Terms for checkers.** Point 3 ("not employment") is right but contradicts the page's headline hooks if a CTA says "earn" or "get paid". Fixes: replace every "earn", "get paid", "income", "gig" with "receive test ADA (no monetary value)" or "try a check"; no hourly or per-task EUR amounts for workers anywhere (the market note lists worker pay of competitors; never imply Shelfie pays that); point 2 should state that test ADA cannot be exchanged for money; point 6 "we may refuse or reverse a payout": a payout on-chain cannot be reversed, say "we may withhold a payout before it is sent". Add adults-only, no entry to staff-only areas, and an explicit line that Shelfie has not asked any store for permission to photograph and that taking photos in a store may be restricted by house rules (Hausrecht) and, in Germany, by the personality rights of persons shown: this is an open question in qa-prep, so the Terms must not promise that photographing is allowed. Place of jurisdiction: drop the placeholder; for consumers statutory rules apply anyway.

**"Earn" with test ADA.** Do not use "earn" at all. Misleading-commercial-practice risk (UWG) is small for a hackathon, but a judge or regulator reading "earn" next to a payout receipt would read it as income. Use "receive 3 test ADA" and keep "no monetary value" adjacent, not in a footnote.

**TUM name and logo.** Use of the factual name is fine ("Built at the TUM Blockchain & AI Hackathon 2026"; "TUM students" for the team bios). Do not use the TUM logo or wordmark on the page unless the organisers' media kit permits it in writing; the Impressum disclaimer ("student project, not an official product of TUM or the organisers") should sit in the footer of every page, not only the Impressum. Also check the hackathon's sponsor/partner logos (Masumi, Cardano Foundation, Sokosumi) on the same basis: text mentions with an "built on" line are enough.

## 4. Positions on trade-offs

1. Receipts labels: keep the five rows but group by Task and label each row with what it is and is not. The headline over the table: "Real test transactions from separate runs. No single job has yet gone through the whole chain with a verified real photo." This sentence goes above the table, not below, and the pitch and the page must say the same thing. When the ADR-20 Task settlement is logged with a real photo, replace the settle row and update the sentence.
2. Derived $1.1T: do not show it on the page. Use $1.7T and "about two thirds out-of-stocks". If the team insists on a single large number for the pitch slide, write "about $1.1T (our arithmetic: 65.6% of IHL's $1.7T)". The competing splits (66.9%, $1.157T, $690.9B empty shelves) make an unlabelled $1.1T attackable.
3. US-only studies: label every one with "US" or "US vendor price list" in the same sentence as the number (T-ROC, Shop!, Kimberly-Clark, McKinsey's 72%). Do not place a US figure next to a EUR figure as if comparable; show "US: $30-45" and "Germany: from about EUR 50" as two separate lines without converting or computing a saving. The McKinsey 59% is global; the 72% is US; say so.
4. Hero: prefer option 1 (no number) or option 2 with the correct McKinsey wording (spend, not failure); do not use option 3 (Shop!).
5. Pricing: EUR 12 and EUR 3.50 are hypotheses; keep "illustrative" in the same line, and drop the "65% margin" from the public page (it rests on an unconfirmed 5% escrow fee).
6. Add one "Limits" line to the home itself (not only trust.html): "Prototype on a test network. The demo runs both sides of the verifier payment. One photo proves price and presence, not placement."
7. Before freezing: open McKinsey and Beroe in a normal browser, confirm the two quoted sentences, and record the date; I could not open either page body today.
